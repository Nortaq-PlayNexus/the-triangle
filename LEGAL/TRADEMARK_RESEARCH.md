# Trademark / IP Research: “Dreamcore”

**Status:** preliminary research only; not legal advice.  
**Access date:** 2026-09-24.  
**Jurisdiction note:** this document is written for an Australian project context but does not determine Australian, US, EU, UK, or other rights.

## Executive caution

“Dreamcore” is already used in at least three different ways:

1. as a community/internet aesthetic;
2. as the title of a released game; and
3. as a word in other game titles and project names.

That does not, by itself, establish that a proposed descriptive use is unlawful or that any party owns the word. It does mean that a logo, badge, directory name, domain, or game title should not be selected without a current professional search.

This project does **not** recommend trying to monopolise a generic genre term. It proposes using “Dreamcore” descriptively with a clear definition, while keeping naming options open.

## Terms distinguished

| Term | Meaning in this project | Example of risk/issue |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive genre terminology | A label that explains a category | “A proposed provenance-based category” is lower-risk framing than presenting the word as an exclusive brand |
| Descriptive use | Using a word to identify a quality, feature, or category | May still create search or marketplace confusion if a project title is identical |
| Game title | The name of a particular game | *Dreamcore* is already a game title on Steam and an official site |
| Studio/company name | A name identifying a business | “Dreamcore Studios” or similar names would require entity and trademark checks |
| Commercial brand | A name, logo, tag line, or badge used to distinguish goods/services | Potentially registrable depending on use, jurisdiction, classes, and distinctiveness |
| Trade mark | A sign capable of distinguishing goods or services of one enterprise from another | WIPO provides this general definition; legal availability must be searched professionally |

- The official game site uses *Dreamcore* as the project identity: [dreamcoregame.com](https://dreamcoregame.com/).
- Press Over's 2023 interview describes the Argentine project and its Backrooms/creepypasta/Stanley Parable influences: [interview](https://pressover.news/entrevista/dreamcore-videojuego-argentino-fusiona-stanley-parable-the-backrooms/).
- *Dreamcore: Rabbit Hole* uses the word as an aesthetic reference in its official Steam description: [Steam appdetails](https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=4149700&l=english&cc=us).

- A research lead points to a USPTO TSDR record, serial `sn99825657`, for a DREAMCORE application associated with downloadable game software in Class 009: [TSDR record](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn99825657). The direct record was not retrievable in this pass; do not rely on its status without a fresh official check.
- itch.io search results use “Dreamcore” across games, assets, music, and other projects: [search](https://itch.io/search?q=dreamcore). This is evidence of marketplace use, not a legal determination.

## Official research routes

- [IP Australia — Trade marks](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks): explains searching existing rights and applying for a mark.
- [Australian Trade Mark Search](https://search.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademarks/search/quick): official search tool.
- [WIPO — Trademarks](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks): general international trademark information and search links.
- [USPTO — Search our trademark database](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search): US search and clearance guidance.
- [USPTO TSDR — serial sn99825657](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn99825657): specific record requiring fresh verification.

A professional search should cover exact words, close spellings, phonetic variants, relevant Nice/Class categories, dead/pending/live records where available, domains, studio names, game titles, and marketplace use. Search results should be saved with an access date and query method.

## Questions for an Australian IP lawyer/trade-mark professional

1. Is “Dreamcore” a registrable or protectable mark for games, software, media, education, community directories, or merchandise in Australia?
2. What is the current status, owner, classes, territory, and goods/services description of USPTO serial `sn99825657`, and are there related Australian or international filings?
3. Are there live or pending Australian filings for “Dreamcore”, “Dream Core”, “Dreamcore Horror”, close variants, or relevant classes?
4. What is the risk of using the word descriptively in a genre article, directory, badge, and press kit?
5. Does a proposed project title such as *THE TRIANGLE: A Dreamcore Horror Game* create avoidable confusion with the existing game?
6. Should a future initiative use a qualified descriptor, a different working name, or a separate brand?
7. What coexistence, attribution, or permission approach would be appropriate if the community wishes to use the existing term descriptively?
8. Are there consumer-law, passing-off, misleading-conduct, or platform-policy concerns?
9. What clearance is needed before registering a domain, logo, social account, or service?
10. What wording should be used when referring to the existing game and its creators?

## Safe interim approach

- Use “Dreamcore” in lowercase/generic explanatory prose, not as a claim of ownership.
- Put the proposed definition beside the first use on every public page.
- Use “proposed,” “working term,” or “research category” until the status changes.
- Do not create a badge that looks like a platform seal or implies certification.
- Do not file, register, publish, or approach the existing game's creators without explicit approval and legal review.
- Keep `GENRE/ALTERNATIVE_NAMES.md` open and do not select a replacement automatically.

## Research conclusion

The name has a real collision profile. The initiative can continue as a transparent research project using descriptive language, but any public-facing brand, logo, directory, domain, or trademark decision requires current Australian professional review.
