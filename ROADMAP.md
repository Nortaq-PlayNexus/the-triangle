# Dreamcore Initiative Roadmap

**Snapshot:** 2026-09-24  
**Operating rule:** stages may advance only when their evidence and approval gates are satisfied. No public outreach or registration is implied by this roadmap.

## PHASE 1 — Research

**Goal:** determine what is already called Dreamcore and whether related practices exist.

**Status in this package:** first documented pass complete; deeper repeat searches remain.

**Deliverables:**

- platform and terminology landscape;
- direct creator examples;
- source index and access dates;
- search limitations;
- contradiction/uncertainty log.

**Exit gate:** a future reviewer can reproduce the main findings and understands that “not found” is not proof of absence.

## PHASE 2 — Definition

**Goal:** define Dreamcore and Dreamcore Horror without broadening the category to fit every dreamlike game.

**Status:** draft definitions, criteria, FAQ, and v1.0 specification written.

**Exit gate:** definitions distinguish provenance, materiality, aesthetic, narrative, mechanics, and genre; privacy is explicit.

## PHASE 3 — Documentation

**Goal:** create a public-facing but currently internal documentation package.

**Status:** this package is an additive first version.

**Exit gate:** all important claims link to local paths or external source IDs; drafts are clearly marked unpublished.

## PHASE 4 — THE TRIANGLE prototype

**Goal:** repair the UE installation, validate the two-environment vertical slice, and preserve evidence of the actual experience.

**Current state:** source work is in progress; the documented UE 5.8.2 installation gate blocks a trustworthy current build/PIE claim.

**Exit gate:** a clean build and recorded slice acceptance covering bedroom → station, lucidity, recognition, manifestation, motif, false wake, save/load, HUD, and audio behavior.

**Guardrail:** do not alter gameplay merely to make it fit Dreamcore.

## PHASE 5 — Public reveal

**Goal:** show THE TRIANGLE as a dream-horror game, with the genre context clearly marked as a proposal.

**Status:** not scheduled; requires creator approval.

**Exit gate:** approved public build/trailer, fact-checked page, privacy-safe provenance statement, and legal review of naming.

## PHASE 6 — Developer/community outreach

**Goal:** invite voluntary participation by creators whose works genuinely meet the definition.

**Status:** guidelines drafted; no contact made.

**Exit gate:** submission form, consent language, review rubric, moderation plan, and anti-spam policy approved.

## PHASE 7 — Press outreach

**Goal:** offer journalists a factual story about research, uncertainty, and a proposed provenance-based category.

**Status:** drafts only; no press contact made.

**Exit gate:** press materials pass anti-hype review and distinguish *Dreamcore* the existing game/aesthetic from the proposed descriptive category.

## PHASE 8 — First external adoption

**Goal:** observe independent, voluntary use of the term in a creator-controlled public context.

**Status:** no verified adoption.

**Exit gate:** at least one informed, consented external use recorded with source, date, and definition context. A single use is an event, not proof of a genre.

## PHASE 9 — Genre directory

**Goal:** maintain a small, evidence-backed directory of candidate and confirmed works.

**Status:** schema drafted; directory not public.

**Exit gate:** moderation policy, consent/takedown process, source retention, versioning, and legal review exist. A directory must not imply that every tag or aesthetic match qualifies.

## PHASE 10 — Long-term preservation

**Goal:** preserve definitions, source records, version history, and contributor consent over time.

**Status:** not started.

**Exit gate:** repository ownership, backups, source access policy, correction process, and periodic review are documented. Preserve contrary evidence and do not rewrite history.

## Decision checkpoints

Before any phase involving publication, contact, money, accounts, domains, badges, or trademarks, obtain explicit approval. Before expanding the definition, test whether existing examples still classify consistently. Before describing adoption, require independent public evidence rather than internal enthusiasm.
