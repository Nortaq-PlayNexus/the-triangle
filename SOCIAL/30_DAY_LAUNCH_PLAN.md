# 30-Day Documentation Launch Plan — Draft

**Status:** planning only. This is not permission to publish, create accounts, contact anyone, or spend money.

The “launch” below means a possible sequence of approved public documentation posts, not a game release or a manufactured genre launch.

## Preconditions

Before day 1:

- current UE build gate and status are fact-checked;
- creator approves public provenance wording;
- no legal conflicts are being presented as resolved;
- source links and access dates are current;
- captions, content notes, and privacy policy are ready;
- every post has an approval owner.

## Days 1–5 — explain the question

- Day 1: Why investigate a category for games made from creators' own dreams?
- Day 2: Dream aesthetic vs dream narrative vs dream mechanics.
- Day 3: The proposed provenance/materiality test.
- Day 4: Why “Dreamcore” needs a careful definition.
- Day 5: Privacy-first participation invitation.

## Days 6–10 — show the evidence

- Day 6: Direct creator examples from itch.io, with exact wording and caveats.
- Day 7: Existing *Dreamcore* game/aesthetic collision.
- Day 8: Adjacent terms: liminal spaces, Backrooms, surreal horror, autobiographical horror.
- Day 9: Academic work on dream narratives and game play/dreams.
- Day 10: Platform-search limitations and why counts are not adoption.

## Days 11–15 — document THE TRIANGLE

- Day 11: The reported personal night-terror basis, privacy-safe.
- Day 12: Bedroom and Platform 12 blockout.
- Day 13: Lucidity and Recognition.
- Day 14: Dream Director, hum, and false awakenings.
- Day 15: Honest build/acceptance status.

## Days 16–20 — invite critique

- Day 16: Ask whether the base definition is too broad.
- Day 17: Ask whether “material” is clear.
- Day 18: Ask how a collective team should be handled.
- Day 19: Ask what alternative terminology feels clearer.
- Day 20: Publish a correction summary if feedback changes the draft.

## Days 21–25 — creator participation

- Day 21: Submission form walkthrough.
- Day 22: How to write a privacy-safe source description.
- Day 23: How classification confidence works.
- Day 24: How correction and withdrawal work.
- Day 25: Share only approved voluntary submissions; do not imply a quota.

## Days 26–30 — close and preserve

- Day 26: Publish the versioned specification changelog.
- Day 27: Archive approved source links and access dates.
- Day 28: Record unresolved questions and negative findings.
- Day 29: Legal/naming research status update.
- Day 30: Publish a transparent “what we learned / what we do not know” summary.

## Prohibited actions during the plan

- No unsolicited creator or journalist outreach.
- No mass tags, DMs, or community posting.
- No fake accounts, reviews, votes, or adoption numbers.
- No domain, social account, badge, or trademark registration.
- No gameplay claim without a verified build.
- No use of private dream details without consent.

## Success condition

The plan succeeds only if readers can accurately explain the proposal, understand its uncertainty, and make a voluntary, privacy-safe decision about whether to participate. Reach, likes, and follower counts are secondary and must not be used as evidence of genre adoption.
