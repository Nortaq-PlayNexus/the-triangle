# Draft About Page

## Why this initiative exists

THE TRIANGLE's creator reports developing a horror game substantially informed by personal night-terror experiences. That personal source prompted a question: could interactive works made from creators' actual dreams and nightmares be described with a useful, respectful vocabulary?

The answer is not yet “yes” as an established genre. The project is researching existing practices and testing a strict definition before any public claim of novelty.

## What we are doing

- Comparing existing uses of *Dreamcore*, dream-game, nightmare-game, liminal-horror, and related terms.
- Separating visual resemblance from documented creative provenance.
- Developing a versioned classification rubric.
- Documenting candidate works without requiring private disclosure.
- Building a voluntary directory and press/research package.
- Auditing THE TRIANGLE without changing its gameplay to fit a label.

## What we are not doing

- Claiming the creator invented Dreamcore.
- Claiming a game is the first of its kind.
- Calling a platform tag a genre.
- Publishing private dream accounts.
- Registering a trademark or domain.
- Contacting developers or journalists without approval.

## Evidence standard

Every important claim should link to a primary source where possible, include an access date, and preserve conflicting evidence. “Not found” means not found in the documented search window.

## Governance principles

Voluntary participation, creator consent, privacy, correction, and transparency take priority over directory size.
