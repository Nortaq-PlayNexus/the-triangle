# History — Draft Page

## What can be said

The research found an existing internet aesthetic called Dreamcore, a released game titled *Dreamcore*, and several games that creators describe as based on real dreams, dream journals, or recurring dreams.

It did not establish:

- the first use of the word Dreamcore;
- the first game matching the proposed definition;
- a formal Dreamcore Horror genre;
- a public announcement or independent adoption of this proposal.

## Selected dated references

- 1998: *LSD: Dream Emulator* used a staff member's real dream diary; it is not confirmed as the game creator's own dream source.
- 2008/2018: *The Majesty of Colors* original/remaster context and first-person dream framing.
- 2018: *#21: The World* creator page states it is based on a personal dream journal. Wu's 2022 research reports a 2018 online antecedent for Dreamcore, while later research says the aesthetic's origin is unclear.
- 2014: *Neverending Nightmares* official metadata describes mental-illness inspiration.
- 2022–2023: The existing *Dreamcore* developer blog dates the project beginning to June 2022; Press Over describes Backrooms/creepypasta influences.
- 2024–2026: *Clockwork Dreams* adds a direct game-wide personal-dream statement; *OMORI* supplies a mixed dream/nightmare case; *Lucid Falls* supplies a concept-level case.
- 2025: Official Steam metadata gives *Dreamcore* a 23 January release date.
- 2026: This project documents a proposal, not an established genre.
