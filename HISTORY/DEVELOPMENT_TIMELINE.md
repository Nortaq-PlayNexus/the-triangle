## Broader landscape chronology (careful)

- **1998:** *LSD: Dream Emulator* is documented as using a staff member's decade-long dream diary. It is an early dream-diary case, not evidence that the diary belonged to Osamu Sato.
- **2008:** Original web-game context for *The Majesty of Colors*; the current remaster page shows a 2018 release and opens with a first-person dream statement.
- **2014:** *Neverending Nightmares* official Steam/Nintendo metadata describes inspiration from the developer's mental-illness struggles; this is adjacent autobiographical horror, not documented personal dream provenance.
- **2018:** *#21: The World* page states it is based on the creator's own dream journal and shows a 2 February 2018 update. Wu's 2022 research reports a 2018 online antecedent for the Dreamcore aesthetic, but the source chain is not definitive.
- **2020–2024:** Academic and community accounts place Dreamcore's broader circulation across liminal-space development and later TikTok-era aesthetics; the exact coinage event remains unresolved.
- **2022–2023:** The official *Dreamcore* developer blog says the project began in June 2022; Press Over's 31 August 2023 interview describes Backrooms/creepypasta influences.
- **2023:** *The Majesty of Colors* remaster page displays 28 February 2018; the 2023 interview is the earliest located public project reference for the game title *Dreamcore* in this pass, not proof of first use.
- **2024–2026:** Steam/itch pages show continued use of “Dreamcore” for liminal, nostalgic, surreal, and dreamlike games, as well as assets and music. *Clockwork Dreams* provides a direct game-wide personal-dream statement; *OMORI* and *Lucid Falls* add mixed and concept-level cases.
