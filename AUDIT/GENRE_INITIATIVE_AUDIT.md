# Final Audit: Dreamcore Initiative

**Audit date:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** research, definitions, history, evidence, public drafts, community infrastructure, legal research, and THE TRIANGLE audit.  
**Nature:** non-destructive audit; no gameplay or existing file was altered.

## 1. Is Dreamcore already an established gaming genre?

**Answer:** No evidence was found that it is a stable, formal gaming genre with a shared industry definition. The word is already established as an internet/community aesthetic and as a released game title. It is also used loosely by stores, creators, tags, and search results. The strongest responsible description is **existing aesthetic/term with a proposed new provenance-based category layered on top**.

## 2. Is Dreamcore Horror already an established gaming genre?

**Answer:** No authoritative platform, academic, or industry source was found that defines “Dreamcore Horror” as a formal genre. The phrase is a reasonable proposed branch, but it must not be presented as an existing standard. Search results and journalism use adjacent language such as psychological horror, liminal horror, analog horror, surreal horror, and nightmare games.

## 3. What existing concepts overlap?

- Dreamcore internet aesthetic.
- Liminal-space and Backrooms games.
- Surreal and psychological horror.
- Dream narratives and dream simulation games.
- Lucid-dream and false-awakening mechanics.
- Autobiographical games about illness, trauma, memory, or family.
- Nightmare fiction and horror.
- Game-jam tags for dreams, nightmares, horror, and weird/surreal work.
- Academic work on dreams as interactive-narrative devices and on game play affecting dreams.

## 4. What is genuinely distinctive about the proposed definition?

The proposed contribution is not the idea that games can depict dreams. It is a **provenance-and-materiality test**:

1. the source is the creator's own dream experience; and
2. that experience materially changes a central part of the interactive work.

That is narrower than “dreamlike,” “surreal,” “liminal,” or “about dreams.” It also permits symbolic transformation and protects creator privacy through abstracted evidence.

## 5. What existing games appear to satisfy the definition?

Direct creator statements support high-confidence or probable matches for:

- *Clockwork Dreams* — creator says the game is based on personal dreams and contains more than 30 dreams/nightmares.
- *#21: The World* — based on the creator's own dream journal.
- *Dream Diary* — created from a real dream.
- *Zemblini: Dream Journal* — based on real dreams.
- *My Magical Dream Journal* — based on true dreams; horror branch probable.
- *Archive Equinox* — travels to locations the creator has dreamed about.
- *My Nightmares Tell Me to Chase My Dreams* — one level explicitly visualizes a recurring dream; the prototype is a partial rather than universal work-level match.
- *The Majesty of Colors* — probable based on the first-person dream framing, pending fuller provenance verification.
- *OMORI* — substantial dream/lucid-dream/nightmare input across major spaces, but mixed with substantial non-dream influences.
- *Lucid Falls* — creator says the core idea came from his dreams; the extent in the finished game is not established.

These are examples of personal-dream practice, **not adoption of this project's proposed label**.

## 6. What games merely share the aesthetic?

- *Dreamcore* by Montraluz/Tlön: official material emphasizes liminal spaces, Backrooms, exploration, and atmosphere; no personal dream provenance is documented in the sources reviewed.
- *Dreamcore: Rabbit Hole*: explicitly says it loves the dreamcore aesthetic and focuses on liminal spaces/1980s ideal America.
- *Explore Dreams: Dreamcore*: uses Dreamcore, vaporwave, and nostalgicore as style references.
- *Neverending Nightmares*: adjacent autobiographical psychological horror based on the developer's mental-illness struggles; the cited official description does not establish a personal dream source.
- *LSD: Dream Emulator*: a real dream-diary game, but a 2024 interview says the diary belonged to a staff member, not Osamu Sato.
- *Happy Game*: writer explicitly rejects personal fear/dreams about evil demons as the main motivation; general dream variety and creative influences are documented instead.

## 7. What terminology conflicts exist?

- “Dreamcore” is an existing aesthetic/community term.
- “Dreamcore” is an existing game title.
- “Dreamcore: Rabbit Hole” and “Explore Dreams: Dreamcore” add title collisions.
- “Dream game,” “nightmare game,” “surreal horror,” “liminal horror,” and “lucid-dream game” already have overlapping uses.
- The proposed term is descriptive, not demonstrated to be exclusive or registrable.

## 8. What evidence exists for historical origin?

**For THE TRIANGLE:** the user brief states a personal-night-terror basis; the Git history documents the repository from 14 September 2026; no dated personal source record or public announcement is present.

**For the broader landscape:** *LSD: Dream Emulator* is an early dream-diary case, but its diary belonged to a staff member rather than Osamu Sato. *#21: The World* has a direct 2018 page statement, and *Clockwork Dreams* now provides a direct game-wide personal-dream statement. Press Over documents the existing *Dreamcore* project in 2023; the official developer blog says it began in June 2022; the existing game's official release metadata is dated 2025. Wu (2022) reports a 2018 Dreamcore antecedent, while later aesthetic research says the origin is unclear. These dates do not establish global priority or a single coinage event.

## 9. What claims can safely be made publicly?

- “Dreamcore is already used as an internet aesthetic and as a game title.”
- “This project proposes a provenance-based category for interactive works derived from creators' own dreams.”
- “Several creators have publicly described games based on real dreams or dream journals.”
- “THE TRIANGLE is a pre-alpha first-person psychological dream-horror prototype; its creator reports personal night-terror provenance.”
- “No independent adoption of the proposed definition was verified in this research window.”
- “The initiative is asking whether a coherent category can emerge, not declaring that one already exists.”

## 10. What claims should not be made?

- “The first Dreamcore game.”
- “The first Dreamcore Horror game.”
- “I invented Dreamcore.”
- “The industry has never seen a game based on a dream.”
- “Dreamcore Horror is an established genre.”
- “Developers are adopting the genre” without dated independent evidence.
- “Dreamcore is owned by this project.”
- Any legal conclusion, clearance, or trademark status.

## 11. What needs legal review?

- Use of “Dreamcore” as a descriptive category, directory, badge, or brand.
- Any game title containing “Dreamcore” or “Dreamcore Horror.”
- Logos, domains, social handles, and merchandise.
- Australian, US, EU, UK, and other relevant trade-mark searches and classes.
- The reported USPTO application serial `sn99825657` and any related family members; current status must be checked directly.
- Passing-off, consumer-law, platform-policy, and coexistence concerns.
- The wording and permissions for using creators' statements and sensitive source descriptions.

See `LEGAL/TRADEMARK_RESEARCH.md`.

## 12. What needs more research?

- Earliest documented origin of the Dreamcore aesthetic, including verification of the 2018 antecedent and the competing 2019/2020 accounts.
- Full first-party history for *LSD: Dream Emulator* and other early examples.
- Creator interviews and primary records for *OMORI*, *Lucid Falls*, *Clockwork Dreams*, and other candidate works, with consent.
- Repeat searches in Epic, GOG, IGDB, MobyGames, RAWG, YouTube, Reddit, and game-development communities using authenticated/API access where appropriate.
- A broader study of autobiographical and personal-experience game design.
- Current Australian and international trade-mark clearance.
- A public definition-consistency test with independent creators.

## 13. What is the minimum viable public launch?

A responsible minimum launch would include:

1. one plain-language page defining the proposal and its limits;
2. the v1.0 specification and criteria;
3. a source/method page with access dates and limitations;
4. a privacy-safe statement from THE TRIANGLE's creator;
5. a small number of clearly labelled candidate examples, not a manufactured “adoption” count;
6. a submission form that does not require private dream disclosure;
7. a correction/takedown process;
8. legal review before using a distinctive logo, domain, or trademark claim.

No public launch is required for the documentation work to remain honest.

## 14. What would need to happen before it could reasonably be described as an emerging genre?

- Multiple independent creators voluntarily use the term or a clearly defined successor term.
- They use it consistently with the provenance-based criteria, not only for aesthetics.
- Public examples show both shared criteria and meaningful variation.
- Critics, developers, and researchers discuss and refine the category without being fabricated or coordinated.
- A transparent directory preserves evidence, uncertainty, consent, and corrections.
- The term's naming/legal conflicts are reviewed and managed.
- The category survives disagreement without being broadened to include every dreamlike game.

## Strongest evidence supporting the concept

- Direct creator statements from several games describe real dreams, dream journals, or recurring dreams; *Clockwork Dreams* and *#21: The World* are especially clear game-wide examples.
- *OMORI* demonstrates that dream, lucid-dream, nightmare, and personal-memory inputs can coexist with other influences, requiring a nuanced rather than binary test.
- The proposed provenance/materiality distinction is coherent and can be applied without requiring private details.
- THE TRIANGLE contains implemented systems that map well to a dream-horror experience.
- A voluntary, evidence-led directory could make the category useful to independent creators.

## Strongest evidence against novelty

- Dreamcore is already an established aesthetic/community term and a game title.
- Personal dream-derived interactive works predate the current proposal.
- *LSD: Dream Emulator* shows that “dream-diary game” does not necessarily mean “creator's own dreams.”
- *Neverending Nightmares* and *Happy Game* show why dream/nightmare aesthetics and personal experience cannot be conflated.
- No standard, repeated independent usage, or verified adoption currently exists.
- The field already has overlapping terms that can absorb the same works.

## Exact current status

> **Dreamcore is a proposed, voluntary, provenance-based game category, currently colliding with an existing aesthetic and game title. Dreamcore Horror is a proposed horror branch, not an established genre. THE TRIANGLE is a provisional candidate based on a creator-reported personal-nightmare source and strong dream-horror design/code evidence.**

## Recommended next steps

1. Repair the Unreal installation and complete a real vertical-slice acceptance run.
2. Create a private, consented provenance map for THE TRIANGLE.
3. Repeat platform research and verify early examples.
4. Obtain Australian IP review before any distinctive public branding.
5. Keep all public language qualified until independent, voluntary use is observed.
