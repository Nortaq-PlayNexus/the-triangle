# GAME DESIGN

## Core loop

Explore → observe → remember → recognize → question reality → become lucid → manipulate the dream → encounter recurring entities → discover connections → lose certainty → return to previous environments → confront the original nightmare.

No weapons, no conventional combat. Survival is observation, exploration, dream manipulation, puzzles and awareness.

## The Triangle

Never a conventional monster. Rarely fully visible. Manifestations vary: a tiny point, a triangular shape, a sharp silhouette, an impossible shadow, an absence where something should be. The player should sometimes know it is present **before** seeing it — via sound, silence, lighting, camera behaviour, a recurring musical motif.

The Triangle stays ambiguous: supernatural, psychological, symbolic, a dream construct — or something else. Never explained.

## Horror style

Anticipation, dread, impossible spaces, recognition, repetition, silence, distorted familiarity, things appearing where they shouldn't, subtle wrongness. The player should frequently think *"Something is wrong"* before they know why. Jumpscares are not the tool.

## Lucidity

Hidden/visible 0..100 meter, event-gated (never a passive timer):

- **Low (0–30):** the dream is accepted; impossibilities pass unnoticed.
- **Medium (30–60):** repeated locations get noticed; doors misbehave; memory of other places returns.
- **High (60–90):** the player can manipulate dream elements; previous environments bleed into the current one.
- **Maximum (90–100):** dream rules break; the Triangle becomes unmistakable; spatial continuity fails.

High-lucidity means the door eventually opens — but the dream decides how it opens.

## Wake mechanic

Not a health bar. Certain actions destabilise the dream: closing eyes, confronting a threat, walking toward danger, focusing on a remembered real object, recognizing impossible geometry. Waking shows screen distortion, audio collapse and a hard cut to a bedroom — but waking is **not always real** (false awakenings).

## Environments (8)

1. Childhood bedroom — the anchor; familiar but subtly wrong; the upper corner draws the eye.
2. Underground train station — fluorescent dread; Platform 12; the train is wrong.
3. Clock room — a giant clock; near 12 everything hushes and shifts.
4. Marble mansion — safest… until rooms change size and doors redirect.
5. Castle — home of the Floating Man, who leads instead of chasing.
6. Witches — two figures who manipulate and contradict, challenging the dream's reality.
7. False-awakening bedroom — layered awakenings, each subtly different.
8. Final fused dream — all environments at once, on Platform 12, the original nightmare.

## Puzzles (dream logic)

Clock synchronisation, impossible architecture, repeated rooms, noticing environmental differences, remembering previous dream places, deliberately breaking dream rules, using lucid abilities, identifying false awakenings, following the Floating Man, interpreting the notebook. Nothing "videogame-puzzle" — everything reinforces the dream.

## The notebook

Fills as the player explores. Drawings, symbols, environment sketches, observations, dates, strange statements. Eventually it contains drawings of places the player hasn't visited yet — and one page with only a triangle. Interacting with it asks: *"Do you remember the first dream?"*

## Ending

No boss battle. The final Triangle starts as an extremely small point. The game asks the player to approach it. As they do: sound disappears, geometry simplifies, HUD vanishes, camera stills. Black. Silence. *WAKE UP.*

The player wakes in the childhood bedroom, exits the house… and outside is the Marble Mansion. Cut to black: **THE TRIANGLE / DREAM AGAIN.** The menu's *NEW GAME* has become *DREAM AGAIN*.
