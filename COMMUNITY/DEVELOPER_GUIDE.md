# Developer Guide

**Status:** draft adoption system; no directory or outreach is live.

## Purpose

The proposed category should be useful to developers who have made interactive works from their own dreams. It is not designed to force a label onto games that merely look dreamlike.

## Start with the source

A useful submission can identify:

- a specific dream, recurring dream pattern, dream journal, or night-terror experience;
- the part of the game that materially grew from it;
- how the source changed the game's world, narrative, mechanic, entity, event, sound, or rule;
- what you want the public to know and what must remain private.

## The five-question test

1. **Source:** Was the experience yours or your credited team's, rather than a fictional dream or borrowed diary?
2. **Materiality:** Would a central part of the game change without it?
3. **Interaction:** Is the work meaningfully interactive?
4. **Evidence:** Can a reviewer verify the relationship without demanding intimate details?
5. **Consent:** Do you control the wording and the level of disclosure?

A “yes” to source, materiality, interaction, and a reviewable evidence path is the base target. Horror intention and a frightening source are additionally required for Dreamcore Horror.

## Common patterns

- A short game reconstructing one dream.
- A collection of dreams from a journal.
- A game whose central mechanic comes from a recurring dream rule.
- A game using a nightmare as a source for an entity or event.
- A team project based on a team member's experience, with consent and attribution.

A game does not have to be first-person, surreal, low-poly, set in a liminal space, or use the word “dream” on its store page.

## Evidence without disclosure

Acceptable descriptions include:

- “The level geometry is based on a recurring dream pattern.”
- “The antagonist's observation behavior comes from a documented nightmare.”
- “The false-waking loop adapts a personal sleep experience.”
- “The work is a symbolic reconstruction; the original account is private.”

Avoid requiring a public journal, diagnosis, trauma narrative, exact location, or names.

## Developer rights

Creators retain ownership of their games, stories, and personal experiences. A directory listing is permission to display the submitted description, not permission to republish the game or infer medical/psychological facts. Ask for withdrawal or correction at any time.

## Review process

1. Receive a voluntary submission.
2. Remove unnecessary personal data.
3. Check source, materiality, interactivity, evidence, and consent.
4. Classify as confirmed, probable, candidate, adjacent, not classifiable, or withdrawn.
5. Preserve contradictory evidence.
6. Ask for clarification only with permission.
