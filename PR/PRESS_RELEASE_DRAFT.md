## Draft

An indie game developer is documenting a proposal for a category called **Dreamcore**: interactive works in which a creator's own dream experiences are a documented, material source of the game's world, narrative, mechanics, events, entities, sound, or rules.

The proposal is being developed alongside *THE TRIANGLE*, a first-person psychological horror game about interconnected nightmares, lucid awareness, recognition, false awakenings, and impossible architecture. Its creator reports that the project is substantially informed by personal night-terror experiences.

The initiative is explicitly research-led. “Dreamcore” is already used as an internet aesthetic and as the title of a released game, and the team is not claiming to have invented the term. “Dreamcore Horror” is likewise a proposed branch, not an established industry category.

The project documentation separates games that look dreamlike from works whose creators can point to a personal dream source and show how it materially shaped the game. The goal is to publish a voluntary definition, evidence index, developer submission guidance, and a careful public directory only after the terminology, privacy, and legal questions have been reviewed.

*The Triangle* is currently a pre-alpha vertical-slice prototype. Its current source includes native Unreal Engine systems for a childhood bedroom, an underground station, Lucidity, Recognition, a Dream Director, false awakenings, a procedural hum, save/load, and HUD work. A repaired engine installation and a completed acceptance run are still required before a public release or gameplay claim is made.

No independent adoption of the proposed category is claimed. The initiative is inviting evidence, critique, and voluntary participation rather than manufacturing consensus.
