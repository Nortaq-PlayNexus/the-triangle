# Anti-Hype and Evidence Policy

**Owner:** THE TRIANGLE research/documentation initiative  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-24  
**Applies to:** research, public drafts, press material, community submissions, social drafts, badges, and future directory entries.

## Purpose

The initiative must test whether a coherent category can emerge. It must not manufacture the appearance of a genre, popularity, adoption, novelty, or legal certainty.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Never fabricate a source.** Every external claim gets a URL, access date, source type, and limitation note.
2. **Never fabricate evidence.** Do not invent screenshots, builds, trailers, interviews, quotes, dates, commits, announcements, or player reactions.
3. **Never call something first without evidence.** “First known in this search,” “first documented example found,” and “first globally” are different claims.
4. **Never turn a tag into adoption.** A platform tag such as `dreams`, `horror`, or `dreamcore` is evidence of platform vocabulary only.
5. **Never infer provenance from appearance.** Dreamlike visuals, liminal spaces, surrealism, first-person perspective, and a dream narrative do not prove that a creator used their own dream.
6. **Never misrepresent another developer.** Preserve the source's wording and distinguish creator statement, journalist description, platform metadata, fan discussion, and reviewer opinion.
7. **Never claim trademark ownership or clearance.** “Dreamcore” has existing uses; this project makes no ownership claim.
8. **Never call the term an established standard.** Until independent creators, maintainers, platforms, critics, and researchers use it consistently, call it a proposal or working category.
9. **Never manipulate adoption.** No fake accounts, astroturfing, vote manipulation, review manipulation, coordinated posting, or undisclosed seeding.
10. **Never spam.** No unsolicited messages, mass posts, community infiltration, or repeated link dropping.
11. **Protect sensitive sources.** Do not require private dream journals, trauma details, medical information, or identifiable dream content. Abstract, consented evidence is sufficient.
12. **Preserve contradiction.** If a source conflicts with another, record both, explain the conflict, and lower confidence.
13. **Separate research from promotion.** Research files state what is known; promotional files state only approved, qualified claims.
14. **Do not alter the game to manufacture a label.** Gameplay changes require a separate design decision and must not be justified by a need to make the project fit Dreamcore.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Directly supported by a primary source or local file, with the relevant excerpt/path recorded. |
| **Probable** | More than one independent signal supports the claim, but a material detail is missing. |
| **Candidate** | Plausible and worth investigating; insufficient evidence for a public classification. |
| **Adjacent** | Overlaps thematically or aesthetically but fails at least one mandatory criterion. |
| **Unresolved** | Sources conflict, access is blocked, or the question requires the creator's private knowledge. |
| **Not established** | No evidence was found in the documented research window; this is not a universal negative. |

## Safe public language

Prefer:

- “The project proposes a provenance-based category…”
- “In this documentation set, Dreamcore means…”
- “The available evidence shows…”
- “This game is an example of the proposed definition, subject to creator confirmation.”
- “Dreamcore is also used online as an aesthetic and as a game title.”
- “This is an emerging proposal, not an industry standard.”

Avoid:

- “The first dream game ever.”
- “I invented Dreamcore.”
- “The industry has never seen this.”
- “The world's first Dreamcore Horror game.”
- “Developers are adopting our genre” without dated, voluntary, public evidence.
- “We own Dreamcore.”
- “The game is legally cleared.”

## Review checklist before any future publication

- [ ] Every factual sentence has a source or is explicitly labelled a proposal/opinion.
- [ ] Dates are dates supported by the source, not inferred from search ranking.
- [ ] The creator has consented to any quoted personal statement.
- [ ] The wording does not imply a platform, critic, or community has endorsed the proposal.
- [ ] Existing game titles and creators are named accurately.
- [ ] Search limitations and unresolved contradictions remain visible.
- [ ] An Australian IP professional has reviewed any proposed brand, title, logo, or trademark filing.
- [ ] No outreach, publication, account creation, or spending occurred without explicit approval.
