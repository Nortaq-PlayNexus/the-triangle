# STORY

## Premise

The player is living a sequence of interconnected nightmares. Early on they seem unrelated. Piece by piece — through the notebook, drawn memories, recognition, and the world's growing instability — the player finds that **they were all one dream all along**.

The Triangle is introduced as an antagonist. It is not one. It may be the point where the dream becomes self-aware. Nothing about it is ever settled.

## Ambiguity rules

- Never explain what the Triangle is.
- Never explain the dream's origin.
- The player finishes with theories, not answers.

## Narrative arc beats

1. **Bedroom** — familiar wrongness; the corner draws the eye; the door refuses to open until the player is lucid.
2. **Train** — Platform 12, an empty station, a wrong train; the Triangle is heard before it is seen. (Phase 2)
3. **Clock room** — near 12 the world hushes and shifts; the clock is connected to the station. (Phase 6)
4. **Mansion** — the one "safe" place; it is the least safe; a window shows the station. (Phase 3)
5. **Castle** — the Floating Man guides, not chases. (Phase 4)
6. **Witches** — psychological manipulation; they know the player's memories; they insist waking is impossible. (Phase 5)
7. **False awakenings** — layered, each subtly different. (Phase 7)
8. **The first dream** — everything fused on Platform 12; the train arrives; this is the original nightmare. (Phase 9)
9. **Approach** — walk toward the point; everything falls silent; wake up. (Phase 10)
10. **The door** — wake in the bedroom, walk out… outside is the Mansion. *DREAM AGAIN.* (Phase 10)

## The notebook as story instrument

The player reconstructs the story from fragments. Drawings, photographs, notes, objects, sounds, phrases, symbols — a recurring memory system that refuses exposition dumps. Its final impossible property: drawings of places the player hasn't yet remembered visiting.

## Tone guardrails

Anticipation over jump scares. Silence over music. Ambiguity over explanation. "Something is wrong" before the player knows why. The world must look believable for the impossible to hurt.
